EASY TABLE GUIDE

Hanafuda Koi-Koi, step by step

The short version: match cards from the same month, collect them, and build special groups called Yaku. When you make a Yaku, keep the points or risk them to try for more.

1. What am I trying to do?

Each flower picture belongs to one of 12 months. Every month has 4 cards. Collect matching cards to build valuable groups. You do not match by color or card type—you match the month.

Example: a March cherry-blossom card matches any other March cherry-blossom card.
2. What happens on my turn?

First: tap one card in your hand. If the field has the same month, you capture the pair. Then: the deck flips one card for you and uses the same matching rule. After that, it is the other player’s turn.

Example: play a July card onto a July field card. You keep both. The deck then flips a November card; if November is on the field, you keep that pair too.
3. How does matching work?

With one matching field card, the game takes it automatically. With two, tap the one you want. Three stay stacked together, and the fourth card captures all four. With no match, your card stays on the field.

Example: two May cards are on the field. You play a May card, so you choose which one to take.
4. What are Yaku?

Yaku are special sets that score. Useful sets include Bright cards; Boar-Deer-Butterflies; the 3 Poetry Ribbons; the 3 Blue Ribbons; 5 Animals; 5 Ribbons; or 10 Chaff. Extra cards can improve some sets.

Example: collect Boar + Deer + Butterflies. That is a Yaku, so the game asks whether you want Shobu or Koi-Koi.
5. Shobu means “stop and keep it”

Choose Shobu to end this month safely and bank your points. This is the easy choice when you already have a good score or your opponent is close to a Yaku.

Example: your Yaku is worth 5. Press Shobu, score 5, and start the next month.
6. Koi-Koi means “I want more”

Choose Koi-Koi to keep playing. You may stop later after making a new Yaku or improving one you already have. It is risky: if the other player scores first, their final score doubles and you get 0 this month.

Example: call Koi-Koi with 5 Animals. Capture a 6th Animal later, and you may choose Shobu again.
7. How are points doubled?

Add the Yaku points first. A base score of 7 or more doubles. If the opponent called Koi-Koi and then lost, double the result again. The bonuses multiply.

Example: 10 base points become 20. A failed opponent Koi-Koi doubles 20 again, so you score 40.
8. Special card: the Sake Cup

The September Sake Cup counts as both an Animal and Chaff, so it can help both totals. Moon Viewing and Blossom Viewing are optional 5-point Yaku and begin turned off.

Example: Sake Cup + 4 other Animals makes 5 Animals. The same Cup can also be your 10th Chaff.
9. Strange opening deals

Four cards of one month on the field means shuffle and deal again. Three stay stacked for the fourth to capture. A hand with four of one month (Teshi) or four pairs (Kuttsuki) wins 6 immediately. If both players have an instant win, the dealer wins the tie.

No turn is played for Teshi or Kuttsuki—the month ends immediately.
10. Last turn and an empty round

On your final card you cannot call Koi-Koi; the game banks the new Yaku because there are no cards left to continue. If everyone runs out without a winning Yaku—or a Koi-Koi player never improves—the dealer gets 6 and stays dealer.

Example: your 8th card completes Blue Ribbons. The game takes Shobu for you.
11. Who deals, and who wins?

The dealer is called Oya and goes first. A normal round winner becomes dealer next month. An instant win or empty-round dealer win keeps the same dealer. A match lasts 3, 6, or 12 months; the highest total wins.

Example: in a 3-month match, add your points from months 1, 2, and 3.
12. Tiny memory trick

Play one → match the month → draw one → match again → check for Yaku. That is the whole turn. The table highlights legal choices, so you can learn by tapping.

No match? The card simply stays in the middle for a later turn.
